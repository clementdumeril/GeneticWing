A 20 km task in the thermals of a real day

The thermals come from the archived forecast for that afternoon: their strength and number follow the ICON-D2 surface heat flux and boundary-layer depth under each point, they are born, grow, fade and drift with the forecast wind. Three pilots fly the same task: the motor all the way, and the wind-aware MacCready tactician at two settings.

    All 48 tasks of each day

    Same pilots on 48 random 20 km routes per day, started between 11:00 and 14:30. Arrived means within the time limit (an average of 12.6 km/h).

    Weather: DWD ICON-D2 (2.2 km) from the Open-Meteo historical forecast archive (CC BY 4.0): sensible heat flux, 2 m temperature, the temperature profile to 700 hPa for the convective layer depth (parcel method) and the 925 hPa wind. w* is Deardorff's convective velocity. Thermal shape, spacing and lifetime follow Allen (NASA/TM-2006-214382): a bell-shaped core, a density of 0.6/(zi·r2), 5 to 30 min of life, and sinking air in between that returns their mass flux.

    Aircraft: SB-XC point-mass model with strip-wing loads; the aircraft and the thermal widths are drawn larger than life, and heights are exaggerated (both adjustable). Built by scenarios/weather_view.py in GeneticWing.
